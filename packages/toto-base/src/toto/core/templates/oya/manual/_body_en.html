Getting started

After signing in you land on the Dashboard — a grid of cards, one per module installed on this server. Click a card to open the module. The header at the top of every page takes you back to the Dashboard and to your profile.

  • Modules are grouped into categories; you can collapse or expand the groups.
  • Only modules installed on this server appear — the same rule applies to this manual.
  • The sun or moon icon in the header switches between light and dark mode.
{% if features.socialhub %}

Profile & communities (SocialHub)

Open Profile from the header to see your member page. Its tabs keep each part apart. Overview is what other members see: your details and the communities you belong to, with what you hide from others marked as hidden. Your own page also has tabs that nobody else sees, administrators included: Edit profile, Account (your e-mail address, password, time zone and language), Security (where you are signed in, your recent sign-ins and your key store), Wallet (your mana) and Your data (a copy of your data, and a request to erase your account). Activity holds your upcoming events and the requests waiting for your answer; on another member's profile it shows only those of their upcoming events you may see, beside Overview and Communities. The SocialHub lists all member profiles and communities.

  • Edit profile — your display name, a few words about you, your avatar and phone, and whether other members see your phone number and e-mail address (both hidden until you switch them on); below them, where you live and whether the People map shows you.
  • Language — on the Account tab, pick your preferred language (Polish or English). The whole interface switches immediately and the choice is remembered.
  • Communities — browse communities and apply for membership. An application may require a verification code and references from existing members.
  • References — when someone names you as a reference, the request appears on the Activity tab of your profile; accept or reject it there.

Your plan

A plan decides which apps you can use. Without one you keep your profile, your files, your communities and your wallet — nothing you have made is ever behind a plan, and nothing is deleted if you stop paying. Paid apps stay visible and readable; what a plan unlocks is the ability to create and change things in them.

Your communities decide which plans you can choose. A plan is offered through a community, and the plans page shows you the ones at least one of your communities offers — so if a tier you expected is missing, the community that would offer it is the place to ask. Everyone always has the free plan.

Your communities also decide what you pay. Each one may take a percentage off a plan, and if you belong to several you automatically get the best of them — there is nothing to claim and nothing to renew. The community itself neither pays nor receives anything; it only changes what its members owe.

A plan is charged once a month. If a month cannot be paid nothing is taken away that day — you keep working, and a banner tells you what happened. After a grace period the paid apps become read-only until it clears, and choosing a plan again restores access immediately. Missed months are written off, never billed later.

Metered work — storage, compute, PDF generation — is charged per use on top of a plan, and is listed on How you are charged. See Plans for what each one includes.

{% if features.mana %}

Storage and security mana

Keeping files here draws a little storage mana every day for each gigabyte you hold, and files you keep unencrypted draw security mana as well. Both pools refill by themselves, a little every hour. When a pool is empty the daily charge simply takes what is there: nothing is owed, no case is opened, and nothing you have stored is ever deleted. Encrypting a file stops its security drain and earns some mana back. Your pools, and what is drawing on them, are on your Mana page.

{% else %}

Storage fee

Keeping files on this server costs a small daily fee, charged once a day per gigabyte from the first byte. There is no free allowance — and that is the simpler deal it sounds like: a free band would be per-metric and use-it-or-lose-it, and the way to use someone else's is to ask them to upload your files. What is free here is anything with no price set at all. The current rate and the currency it is charged in are published on the Storage held page, which also shows what you store and what tonight's charge will be. The fee is taken from your account automatically — you are not asked to confirm it. This is a platform rule, the same one that applies to every metered action.

  • If a daily charge fails because your balance is too low, a notice appears in your calendar and in your invites: you have one week to top up. No debt builds up — a day that could not be charged is written off and is never collected later.
  • To close the case, add funds to your wallet (ask an administrator, or trade on the bourse). The next successful daily charge — or deleting enough of your own files that there is nothing left to charge for — resolves it.
  • If the week passes unpaid, your account stops accepting new uploads until the debt clears. Nothing you have stored is ever deleted to settle a bill — not by staff, not automatically. You keep every file you have; you simply cannot add more until you pay or make room.

Time limits work the same way. Some things are bounded by time rather than space — how long an idle Python kernel stays warm, how long a compile, a media job or a workflow step may run. Each has a free default, and you can raise it where you use it, or see all of them together on Extended time held; holding a raised limit costs a small daily fee, charged automatically per hour of extension, whether you use it or not. If the fee cannot be collected, the same warning and one-week rule applies: you cannot raise anything further until it clears, and the limits you have already raised keep working. Housekeeping runs regardless: idle kernels stop automatically once their limit passes (variables are cleared, files are untouched), and background jobs that die without reporting are closed as failed within a few hours.

Nothing here is charged for simply being a member. Every daily fee above is for something you are holding, and it stops the moment you stop holding it. What you pay to be here is a subscription — monthly, and priced before you agree to it. Every way this platform charges is listed on How you are charged.

Locations

Locations is an interactive map of points, territories, routes and addresses. Browse the map, inspect places, and search for routes between locations.

Polls

A poll asks the people in a room a question: pick an option, and watch the count come in. Polls live inside the Forum — open a room and choose its Polls tab, where any member can start one. Some polls let you change your mind while they are open; others take one answer each and show nothing until they close. Either way the result is informational — a poll records what people think, and decides nothing. Formal company decisions are not made here; they are made in Irena and shown, verified, on the Company dashboard.

{% endif %} {% if features.ireneo %}

Company

{% url 'ireneo:overview' as ireneo_url %} The Company dashboard shows who owns the company, who runs it, how it is organised and what it has decided: shareholders with their share of the capital and of the votes — which are not the same number when share classes carry different voting rights — the board with its executive and non-executive members, the departments, and the register of decisions.

Everything on it is a read-only copy. The register of record is Irena, a separate application; this platform receives verified copies and displays them. Nothing here creates a vote, decides a quorum, changes who owns what or alters a decision that has been taken, and there is no route from this dashboard back into Irena's ledger. Being able to open the page is a viewing permission on this platform and confers no authority in the company.

Transfer moves a whole company between platforms, in Irena's own document format. Building a bundle produces a register document — the company as a directory on one day — as a single XML file, or a ZIP with that file alongside Irena's original documents and any files attached to decisions; loading one on another platform reproduces the same dashboard. Both run in the background, and the page lists what happened. Standing comes from signatures, not from who claims to have written a file: a register signed by Irena loads as verified, an unsigned one — including every bundle this platform writes — loads as imported data shown plainly as unverified, and a document whose checks fail is refused.

Compute Gears

{% url 'anastasia:index' as gears_url %} Heavy work does not run on the platform itself. Compiling a document, converting a video, reading text out of a scan, running Python — each of those needs real CPU and memory, and this server hands that out deliberately rather than to whoever asks first. You reserve it in advance on the Compute Gears page. One reservation is called a Gear.

  • Reserve — choose how much CPU, memory, scratch space and how many processes you want, and give it a name. The page shows what is still free in the pool before you ask.
  • Mount — the Gear becomes alive and shows READY. Until you mount it the booking is held but nothing can run in it.
  • Use it — go to the feature you actually want: Compile in TeX Lab, Run in Python Lab, a command in Media conversion, Read in Text recognition. Each job runs in a throwaway container inside your Gear's limits and is destroyed the moment it finishes. While you hold exactly one mounted Gear it is chosen for you. You may hold up to three at once, and from the second one on, a workspace (TeX Lab, Python Lab) has to say which it runs in — open its Settings and pick the Gear there; a Python kernel moves on its next restart, a compile on its next run. Media conversion, Text recognition and the deck export have no workspace to carry that choice yet, so they still need you to hold exactly one mounted Gear.
  • Unmount or release — unmount stops the runtime and keeps the booking; release gives the capacity back to the pool.

A reservation is held whether or not anything is running in it — that is what makes it a reservation and not a queue, and it is why releasing a Gear you have finished with matters. Scratch space is memory rather than disk, so a Gear's real ceiling is its RAM plus its scratch. If a job asks for more than the whole Gear holds, the refusal says so and names the shortfall; if the Gear is merely busy, it says that instead.

Nothing heavy works without a mounted Gear. That is on purpose: a page that quietly borrowed the server's memory because you pressed a button is how one export takes a platform down. When you have none, the button refuses and points you back here.

{% endif %} {% if features.latex %}

TeX Lab

{% url 'texlab:lobby' as texlab_url %} TeX Lab turns a folder in your storage into a LaTeX workspace: a file tree on the left, an editor in the middle, and a Compile button. Multi-file documents work the way you expect — \input{} and \include{} resolve against the workspace, and the whole tree is sent to the compiler together.

Compiling runs latexmk in your Compute Gear, not on the platform, so this server carries no TeX installation at all; the workspace's Settings say which Gear. The compiler log comes back into the room's console, and everything the compile produced — the PDF first of all — is filed into a read-only build/ folder in the workspace, where the file tree on the left lists it like any other file in the bucket. Click the PDF to open it in a new tab in the browser's own viewer; there is no preview pane.{% if features.workflows %} A compile is queued rather than held open, so you can leave the page and come back to it.{% endif %}

{% endif %} {% if features.dracena %}

Python Lab

{% url 'dracena:lobby' as dracena_url %} Python Lab is a workspace you can run code in, notebook style: write a cell, run it, keep the result. Variables persist between runs, errors come back as results rather than as failures, and pictures a cell draws are shown inline.

The interpreter itself is a runner inside your Compute Gear — the platform only speaks the protocol. Closing the page does not lose the session: reconnecting attaches to the same kernel, and Restart gives you a clean one without touching your files. If you keep a kernel warm in your Gear, coming back is instant instead of waiting for one to start; a warm kernel counts against that Gear's reservation like anything else, and dies with it.

{% endif %} {% if features.manta %}

Media conversion

{% url 'manta:command_builder' as manta_url %} The Media conversion desk does the ordinary things to an audio or video file you already have in storage: resize it, compress it, cut a section out, change the frame rate, strip the audio, crop it, or turn a clip into a GIF. You pick the file and the command and fill in its options — you never write a command line.

The conversion runs in your Compute Gear, so this server carries no media tooling itself, and the result is filed back into your storage as a new file. The original is never modified. Longer jobs keep going after you leave the page; the job's own page shows where it got to.

The Knowledge Graph stores objects and their relationships as a graph you can search, edit and analyse. The page is organised into tabs:

  • Query — search the graph or run Cypher queries and explore the results visually.
  • Data — browse and edit the graph: define node categories and edge types, then add and modify nodes and edges.
    • {% if features.workflows %}
  • Analysis — run predefined graph analyses; results are saved to the storage.
    • {% endif %}
  • Knowledge — paste text and let the platform suggest entities and relations; review the proposal on a canvas and apply it to the graph.
    • {% if features.ocr %}
  • OCR — text extracted from an image (see the OCR section) can be handed straight to Knowledge.
    • {% endif %}
  • Objects elsewhere in the platform (for example events or locations) offer an Export to graph button that adds them, with their direct connections, to the graph.
